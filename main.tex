\section{Introduction}
Today, we live in the 21st century, which is the age of technology development and digitization around the world. There are more than 8 billion people living all over the world and each year millions of new users start to use the Internet, and that's why today digital identity is extremely important.

With the growing popularity of online platforms, social networks, gaming services, and web applications, there has been a growing need for unique usernames. Usernames are used as public identifiers which make it possible to differentiate between users of the system. Moreover, usernames are essential for such processes as authentication, customization, social interaction and account management. At the same time, with the growing number of users of platforms, there arises a problem with generation of unique usernames due to the problem of name collision, username squatting and the lack of availability of common names.

To address these issues, various algorithms and data structures have been developed for generation and verification of usernames. Even though database indexing guarantees efficiency and accuracy, using the database alone can make the system inefficient due to an increased workload. That's why additional techniques such as Bloom Filters and distributed hashing mechanisms are used to improve scalability and performance in large-scale systems. Bloom filters are probabilistic algorithms which allow for quick membership tests and reduce database lookups by a huge margin \cite{b1,b2}. Similarly, consistent hashing allows for efficient mapping of user records to multiple servers and greatly reduces data shuffling when new servers join or leave the network \cite{b3}. These are some of the mechanisms that make it possible to deal with username verification more efficiently.

In case of username generation, there are a number of methods used to generate unique and user-friendly usernames. These include Random Suffix Generation where random numbers or alphanumeric characters are appended to the usernames in order to minimize collisions. There is also the Snowflake-based approach which helps in generating unique identifiers in a distributed computing environment and can be applied in the generation of usernames to guarantee uniqueness. Lastly, Trie-based approaches help in prefix search and generation of usernames based on user-specified prefixes such that the user is presented with usernames similar to what he/she wants.

The research focuses on conducting a comparative study of algorithms for generating unique usernames and verifying their uniqueness. Various methods will be examined in terms of their effectiveness, scalability, ability to ensure uniqueness, efficiency, and applicability in large-scale systems. The results of this research can be useful for choosing an appropriate solution to username management for contemporary web applications. 

\section{Literature Review}
Luo et al. presented a detailed introduction of Bloom Filters and the various variations developed to improve their performance \cite{b1}. They studied the basic concepts behind Bloom Filters as well as challenges in terms of false positives, space usage, and limitations due to scaling problems. The work is categorized using two distinct dimensions. First one is performance, where the focus is on the reduction of false-positive rate or on the optimization of insertion and query computation time. Second one is generalization, where the authors explore the way of applying the setup of a Bloom filter  either through the inclusion of new types of input or output. Besides this, the paper also highlights implementation-level optimization tricks, like techniques for generating multiple hashes by using onlyone or two basic hash functions at each hashing step. There are several modifications in Bloom Filters such as Counting Bloom Filters and Scalable Bloom Filters which overcome the aforementioned challenges. It was stated that Bloom Filters offer a good compromise between storage requirements and time to check the membership of items, thus can be used for large-scale systems where rapid checks are required.


Ramabaja and Avdullahu presented the enhanced version of Bloom Filter as the Distributed Bloom Filter \cite{b2}. These researchers tried to find ways of how to improve the scalability and minimize the amount of communication overhead for working with large sets of data in distributed settings. In particular, the Distributed Bloom Filter algorithm includes the distribution of Bloom Filters among different machines while preserving the efficient checking of membership.  It has been shown through experiments that Distributed Bloom Filter can effectively handle large-scale systems without extra computational overhead.

Karger et al. proposed the concept of consistent hashing designed for effective distribution of data on several servers in a distributed environment \cite{b3}. In contrast to standard algorithms of hashing, which involve significant amount of work in redistribution of data when there is a change in number of nodes, Consistent Hashing reduces the need for redistribution and provides load balancing. It was proven that this algorithm increases scalability and improves performance of systems making it very appropriate for large scale storage and retrieval purposes. Nowadays Consistent Hashing is one of the most popular algorithms used in distributed databases, caches and clouds.

Fredkin introduced the Trie as a type of tree-like structure which allows for a really fast storage and retrieval of strings \cite{b4}. It turns out that with the use of Tries, it is possible to perform searches based on prefixes very rapidly. In fact, the time taken to carry out the lookup operation depends mainly on the length of the string being searched for, irrespective of the number of strings that have been stored. Because of this property, Tries are found in a lot of applications such as autocomplete systems, dictionaries, and recommendation engine. In username handling, the Trie-based systems can find different usernames if the original one is already taken quickly.

Chinthareddy presented stateless Snowflake method for generating IDs which is suitable to cloud-native ecosystems \cite{b5}. The problem was without a centralized control or manually assigned worker numbers, there was no way to ensure uniqueness globally. This research managed that problem of creating unique identifiers worldwide without a central control. The proposed solution was to create a unique ID which relies on network-based identity. Also, the method was able to do so with high speed and at the same time keep the system upscaling reliable. The findings showed that the Snowflake method, which is at the heart of generating IDs, is very powerful when applied to large-scale distributed systems. With such a system, collisions can be minimized without compromising the uniqueness of names.

Though earlier work has been done on Bloom Filters, Distributed Bloom Filters, Consistent Hashing, Trie structure, and Snowflake generation technique, most of the work is based on each individual algorithm separately or even in particular areas like distributed storage, caching, authentication mechanism, etc. But there is little work on comparing username generation algorithms and verification algorithms in a collective way. Thus this paper tries to fill this void by performing the comparative study on Bloom Filter, Consistent Hashing, Database index search, Snowflake generation, Trie structure suggestion generation, and Random Suffix Generation techniques.
